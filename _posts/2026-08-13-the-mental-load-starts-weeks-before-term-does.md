It's still August. The uniform hasn't been tried on for size, the shoes aren't bought, and school doesn't start for weeks yet. But somewhere, in the back of one parent's mind, a list is already forming: sizes, deadlines, non-uniform days, seven different school apps and logins to keep straight. As one mum put it on Mumsnet, "Between two schools I have at least seven different apps and logins I need to use/check to book things and find information. It drives me barmy!"

She isn't disorganised. She's holding a dozen small systems that nobody designed to talk to each other, weeks before anyone sets foot in a classroom.

## The scattering is the load

New research from the Universities of Bath and Melbourne, published in the journal Socius (2025), asked 2,133 US parents how much of the household's cognitive load they each carried across 24 everyday tasks. Mothers reported handling 13.72 of them on average, fathers 8.18. Scheduling showed the widest gap of any category: mothers 2.44 tasks to fathers' 0.83, a 194% difference, ahead of both cleaning and childcare.

Back-to-school prep is scheduling in its purest form: dates to remember, forms to send back, kit lists to check against a drawer that may or may not still fit. No wonder it lands so heavily on one person.

## More than one bill

There's a cost beyond time, too. UK parents spend an average of £422 a year on secondary school uniform and £287 for primary, with schools asking for three or more branded items as standard (The Children's Society, 2023). Add the non-uniform days that seem to appear from nowhere. "Remembering the dates, finding outfits, sending money in. It's a steady mental load," as one parent described it. Another said her heart sank every time a dress-up day was announced. The day itself is almost beside the point. What lands is one more thing to notice and get right on somebody else's timetable.

## Why it doesn't feel like "a task"

Researcher Allison Daminger's work on the mental load (American Sociological Review, 2019) helps explain why this is so hard to see, let alone share. Buying uniform in good time is anticipating. Checking whether a form actually got signed is monitoring. Neither shows up as a single job on a to-do list, so neither gets counted, even though both sit in someone's head for weeks. It's little wonder a recent US survey found 57% of parents feel relief and guilt at once when term restarts, and 23% feel overwhelmed by the logistics alone (Talker Research/Rula, 2026). The feeling is real. It just rarely gets named.
